Part 3 · The one idea

A rule in a prompt is a suggestion.
A rule in code is a wall.

Every other agent asks the AI nicely to behave. Apollo doesn't ask. It checks every action the AI wants to take before it happens, in code the AI can't see or change, and either lets it through, blocks it, or buzzes your phone.

THE OLD WAYSecurity by politeness

"You are a helpful assistant. Please be careful and always ask before deleting files or pushing code."

That's it. That's the whole defence. The AI reads it, agrees, and then does whatever seems reasonable in the moment. Including things that aren't.

THE APOLLO WAYSecurity by wall

AI wants to run: git push --force → reversible? NO → inside the allowed folder? irrelevant → verdict: BLOCKED. logged. AI told why. → it never gets to run it. not once.

The AI can want whatever it wants. The wall decides. And the wall keeps receipts.

Part 4 · Seven promises

Each one is a fix for a real scar.

I

It asks before anything it can't undo

Flashing a chip, pushing code, installing stuff: your phone buzzes with the exact command. Tap Allow or Deny from the lock screen. No answer in 10 minutes? That's a no.

II

It lives in one folder and can't leave

It can read and write inside its sandbox. Everything else on the machine is behind a locked door, including its own settings and your passwords.

III

It notices when it's spinning and restarts with a clear head

Same mistake twice? Blocked. Still stuck? It writes itself a short note, wipes its memory of the mess, and starts fresh from the note.

IV

Every action is logged, and the log can't be faked

Each entry is chained to the one before it, so nobody can quietly edit history. Not the AI, not a bug, not a bad day.

V

One big red button

Phone, desktop hotkey, or a single command. Everything stops in under two seconds, and your work is still there because it all happened on a separate branch.

VI

It stays on your machine

Voice in, voice out, all local. No cloud microphone, no Discord bot, no third party in the middle. You reach it from your phone over your own private network.

VII

It doesn't get to say it's right. It has to prove it.

"The file is missing"? Apollo makes it run the command and show the output before the claim counts. "It's fixed"? Nothing is marked done until the build and tests actually pass, and the agent physically can't finish while they're red. A second AI, in a separate room with no memory of the work, grades the result, because every AI grades its own homework generously. And the logbook is written by Apollo, never by the AI, so its version of events is never the only version.

Plain English
The contractor doesn't tell you the wall is load-bearing. He shows you the blueprint, the inspector signs off, and the camera footage is kept by someone else.
